Estimates per cohort.

These estimates come from IRCC's published queue numbers for proof of citizenship, broken down by application month (cohort), going back to Nov 2025. For each cohort you can see how many people who applied that month are still waiting. Across cohorts, you can also see where the processing is happening.

A long-standing question is whether processing is random or first in, first out. Comparing cohorts month by month gives a mix of both. Pooled across all the cohorts, over the last 3 months the score is 0.32, where 0 is random and 1 is strictly oldest first. The model splits processing into three phases:

  • Trickle: files processed out of order, at about 1.4% of a cohort's waiting applicants a month, until the sweep reaches it.
  • Sweep: the oldest-first push, which works through one cohort at a time. When it reaches yours, it processes about 50% of whoever is still waiting when it arrives, in a few months.
  • Leftovers: the rest, whom the sweep passes over. From then on they clear at their own rate, faster than the trickle but far slower than the sweep: about 3.6% a month over the last 5 months. At that rate, half of them are done in about 19 months.

Each row shows when your cohort is in each phase: when the sweep arrives, when it finishes, and when half the leftovers have been processed. The percentage at each point is the chance you've been processed by then.

Only cohorts whose counts have settled are shown. A new cohort's count swings up and back down for its first few months. IRCC's counts are rounded to the nearest 100. The estimates update when IRCC refreshes its feed.

Underlined figures are recalculated each time IRCC publishes new numbers.
